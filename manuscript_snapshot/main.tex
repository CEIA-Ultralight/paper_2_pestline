\keywords{Pest Monitoring, Sticky Traps, Tiny Object Detection, Fine-Grained Classification, Detection Cascade, Data-Centric AI.}

\abstract{Automated species-level monitoring of flies on industrial sticky traps combines tiny objects (mean box size $\approx$44\,px in 1920$\times$1080 images) and fine-grained distinctions between look-alike species. We test three levers on DS-F2, a dataset extended with targeted physical collections. On a ladder of five cumulative training sets, the share of species instances localized and correctly named by a single-stage detector rises from 56\% to 82\% (three seeds), but random subsamples of the final training set match the 244- and 869-image rungs, so up to there we detect no advantage of targeting over quantity (the control is partial). A detect-then-classify cascade does not improve on the detector in any of 15 configurations with our recipe, and ground-truth-crop accuracy saturates near 83\% for five classifiers; limited information in the images is one explanation, but ambiguous pixels, label noise and the crop recipe cannot be separated. Tiny-object methods from aerial imagery (NWD loss and assigner, RFLA, P2 head, higher resolution, sliced inference) give no gain detectable with three seeds and default hyperparameters (NWD with RFLA failed on unstable seeds and is uninformative). Code will be released; the images will not.}
