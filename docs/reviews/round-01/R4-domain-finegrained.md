## 1. Paper Summary

The paper continues a data-centric pipeline for per-species fly counting on industrial adhesive plates (four target classes, mean object side 44 px in native 1920×1080 frames, no downscaling allowed). It poses three questions: RQ1, whether acquisitions targeted at three documented failure modes plus real-instance copy-paste beat volume-driven augmentation (no evidence yet); RQ2, whether a hybrid $(1-w)\,\mathrm{CIoU}+w\,\mathrm{NWD}$ objective in both heads of an end-to-end YOLO26 helps at native resolution (+1.4 pp VAL mAP50, single seed, multi-seed campaign running); and RQ3, whether a detector→crop→ConvNeXt-T-with-part-attention cascade recovers species confusions the detector cannot. For RQ3 the evidence is Tab. 3: on a 55-image / 918-instance test set the cascade raises species recall 75.7→80.1 % and F1 73.2→75.3 % at unchanged precision, while 7-class mAP50 drops 4.7 pp and latency rises 18× (25→463 ms/img). The abstract states this "disproves" the prior work's claim that the flesh-fly/blow-fly boundary is irreducible.

## 2. Strengths

**S1 — The RQ3 finding is framed as a finding, not as a method.** Sec. 2 ¶3 correctly places part attention (MA-CNN, Zheng et al. 2017), SupCon (Khosla et al. 2020) and ArcFace (Deng et al. 2019) as standard FGVC tools and states that the contribution is the observation that (i) part attention transfers to ≈40 px crops, (ii) metric heads do not add on top of it here, and (iii) re-labelling without re-ranking raises operating-point accuracy while lowering AP. This is the right framing for an applied paper and I do not penalise the use of off-the-shelf components.

**S2 — Selection protocol for the headline classifier is clean and stated.** Sec. 1 item 3 and Sec. 6 ¶2 say the part-attention classifier was selected on validation before the test set was consulted, and that the three-classifier ensemble (chosen after seeing test) is demoted to "exploratory". `RELATORIO_TESTE_20260909.md` §1 and §6 confirm this. This is exactly the discipline a pest-monitoring paper needs and is rarely done in the insect-detection literature.

**S3 — Per-species metrics exist in the source data and the paper commits to reporting them.** Sec. 5.3 `\TODO` promises MD 54.3→66.2 recall and MF 78.3→84.4; `PAPER_FACTS.md` §3 and the report §4 have full precision/recall for all 7 classes under both pipelines. For pest monitoring, per-species recall is the quantity inspectors care about, and Tab. 3 already reports recall/precision/F1 over the four species with INS/NOISE/MAR excluded consistently.

**S4 — Negative results in the classifier stage are given mechanisms, not just listed.** Sec. 1 item 4 and Sec. 6 ¶4 attribute the failure of larger crops to "ampliar o recorte não cria detalhe", of SAM cleaning to removal of contextual cues, and of larger backbones to no extra signal at this scale. These match the FGVC intuition that ~40 px objects carry a fixed information budget.

**S5 — Honest limitations.** Sec. 6 states the test set is "consolidated, not blind", that classifier results are single-seed with a second seed several points lower, that two evaluators must never be mixed, and that the 463 ms latency re-classifies *all* 4,197 low-confidence proposals and is therefore an upper bound. This is above the norm for the venue.

## 3. Weaknesses

**Major (must fix before submission)**

1. **The abstract's "disproving the irreducibility claim" is not supported by the paper's own per-species numbers.** The prior work (`pacbb.txt` l. 353–358) declares two boundaries irreducible: Flesh Fly ↔ Blow Fly and Fruit Fly ↔ Noise. In the current coding these are MC ↔ MV and MF ↔ NOISE. `PAPER_FACTS.md` §3 shows the cascade *lowers* MC (flesh fly) recall 85.3→81.2 % and raises MV (blow fly) recall only 83.6→86.9 %; the recall gains that drive the +4.4 pp headline come from MD (housefly, 54.3→66.2) and MF (fruit fly, 78.3→84.4), and MF precision *drops* 79.7→73.5 % — i.e. the cascade likely relabels more debris as fruit fly, which is the second "irreducible" pair going the wrong way. The report §3 also states MD is confused mainly with MV, so the pair the cascade actually helps is housefly ↔ blow fly, which the prior work never called irreducible. Sec. 1 item 3 hedges correctly ("changes the balance between the two confusable classes"), but the abstract (Sec. 0) and Sec. 2 ¶4 ("revisits their claim") overclaim. Fix: replace "disproving" with a precise statement of what happened on MC↔MV, add the pairwise confusion counts before/after (the `\TODO` in Sec. 1 item 3), and separately discuss MF↔NOISE.

2. **Class-code collision between the prior paper and this dataset is not handled and will mislead readers.** PACBB used FF/HF/BF/**MF** with **MF = flesh fly**; DS-F2_v8.1.1 uses MD/MV/MC/**MF** with **MF = fruit fly**. The supplementary skeleton `X_suppl.tex` §"Error analysis MF↔BF / MD↔MV" uses the *old* codes (there is no BF class in this dataset, and MF now means a different insect); `PAPER_FACTS.md` §5 also writes "MF↔BF". If this propagates into Sec. 5.3 or the supplement, the irreducibility discussion will refer to the wrong species. The written sections currently avoid codes for this pair (Sec. 1 ¶1 lists "house fly, blow fly, flesh fly and fruit fly" in MD/MV/MC/MF order, which is correct). Fix: (a) never use PACBB codes in this paper; (b) give a class table in Sec. 3.1 with code, common name and taxon; (c) add one sentence in Sec. 3.1 stating the code mapping to STID explicitly.

3. **The cascade is evaluated only on E0 (CIoU baseline), not on the strongest detector (E10, NWD).** Tab. 3 and `PAPER_FACTS.md` §2–3 use E0 proposals throughout. The paper's thesis (Sec. 1 ¶3: data + tiny-aware detector + cascade "combined") requires showing that the cascade gain survives on E10 — better localisation could shrink the pool of recoverable errors, or the NWD assigner could change the proposal distribution the classifier sees. Without E10+cascade the two halves of the paper (RQ2, RQ3) are additive only by assumption. Fix: run the harness on E10 (and ideally E10b) with the same frozen classifier; report both in Tab. 3.

4. **Absolute size — the strongest family-level cue in a fixed-distance rig — is discarded by the crop pipeline, and this is not discussed.** With a fixed camera and working distance (Sec. 1 ¶1, Sec. 6 ¶1), a housefly (~6–7 mm), a blow fly (~8–14 mm), a flesh fly (~10–15 mm) and a fruit fly (~3 mm) occupy systematically different pixel areas. `fly-det/scripts/extract_crops.py` crops box-relative with 15 % padding and the classifier resizes to 384 px, so scale is normalised away and the classifier must separate MD from MV on texture alone. This is a plausible cause of the MD↔MV residual and of the failure of "higher-resolution crops" (Sec. 1 item 4). The method section (Sec. 4.3, currently `\TODO`) must state the crop policy, and the paper should either ablate a fixed-size context window (e.g. 128×128 px at native resolution, no per-box resize) or feed box size as an auxiliary input. This is a small experiment with a clear FGVC rationale.

5. **Train/inference crop mismatch in the classifier stage.** The classifier is trained on ground-truth crops (`extract_crops.py`) but applied to YOLO proposals down to conf 0.001 (Sec. 6 ¶4), many of which are background or debris. The report §7 itself lists "train on real YOLO proposals including false alarms" as a next step. This mismatch explains the +13 false alarms and the MF precision drop in W1, and it must be stated in Sec. 4.3 as a design limitation, not left to the reader. Also state whether the classifier is 4-class or 7-class (the harness uses 7 classes): if 7-class, a species box can be relabelled NOISE and silently removed from species recall.

6. **The accuracy-up/mAP-down explanation is incomplete.** Sec. 1 item 3 says "the cascade re-labels but does not re-rank"; yet `PAPER_FACTS.md` §2 shows the *product-score* policy, which does re-rank, still loses AP (77.28 vs 81.18 VAL mAP50). The more likely mechanism is that relabelling thousands of sub-0.25 proposals moves low-confidence background boxes into species classes, lengthening the low-precision tail of each species' PR curve. The paper must either give this fuller explanation with a per-class AP breakdown, or restrict re-classification to proposals above the operating threshold (which would also cut the 18× cost) and show what happens to both AP and recall.

7. **Taxonomic rank of the "species" classes is undefined and the difficulty claim is unexamined.** The paper calls MD/MV/MC/MF "four target species" (Sec. 0, Sec. 1), but blow flies (Calliphoridae), flesh flies (Sarcophagidae) and fruit flies (Drosophilidae) are families; only housefly (*Musca domestica*) is a species. Family-level Calliphoridae vs Sarcophagidae is *not* hard for a trained taxonomist at good resolution (metallic blue/green vs grey with three thoracic stripes and a chequered abdomen), so the prior claim that it "challenges even trained human taxonomists" was already doubtful a priori; conversely MD ↔ MV is plausible at 40 px when metallic sheen is dulled by adhesive. Sec. 6 ¶1 still has a `\TODO` for who annotated and how labels were verified. An entomologist would require: rank per class, the morphological cue set used by annotators, and inter-annotator agreement on at least a subset. Without this, neither the "irreducible" claim nor its refutation is well defined.

8. **RQ3 sections are skeletons.** Sec. 4.3 (cascade method), Sec. 5.3 (per-species table, paradox explanation, seed caveat), Sec. 5.5 (negative-results table), Sec. 7 and the supplementary are `\TODO`. In particular, the per-species table promised in Sec. 5.3 is the single most important table for this reviewer's lens and is absent from the PDF. No rating above Borderline is possible until they exist.

2. Sec. 0 says "held-out test set"; Sec. 6 ¶2 says the same set is "consolidated, not blind". Use one wording ("plate-disjoint test set previously consulted in exploratory work") in both places.
3. Sec. 1 item 4 lists "multi-scale training at 1920 px" among things that "fail to beat the simple baseline"; `PAPER_FACTS.md` §1 says E13 was CUDA-OOM and never ran. Say "could not be run" rather than "failed to beat".

5. Sec. 2 ¶4 positioning is fair but thin on sticky-trap cascades. 7. Sec. 6 ¶4: the latency paragraph should say what deployment looks like (plate photographed once per inspection cycle, offline batch on a server?). If so, 463 ms/img is irrelevant in practice and the paper is under-selling the cascade; if edge inference is required, it is a real barrier. Currently the reader cannot tell.

9. Teaser (`fig/teaser.tex`) is the template placeholder with template caption text. For this paper the teaser should show a native-resolution crop of each of the four classes at ≈40 px alongside the plate — that single figure would make the difficulty concrete for non-entomologists.

## 4. Questions for the authors

1. What are the pairwise confusion counts MC→MV and MV→MC on TEST before and after the cascade (from the `confusion/cascade/*` diagnostics listed in `RELATORIO_TESTE_20260909_APENDICE.md`)? Did MC↔MV confusion decrease at all, or did the cascade only move errors between MD and MV?
2. Where do the extra MF (fruit fly) false positives after the cascade come from — NOISE proposals relabelled as MF? If so, does that not *reinforce* the prior paper's second irreducibility claim (fruit fly ↔ sub-millimetre debris)?
3. Is the classifier 4-class or 7-class? If 7-class, how many GT species instances were relabelled to INS/NOISE/MAR by the cascade?
4. What is the crop policy (padding, resize target, aspect handling)? Have you tried a fixed-size native-resolution window so that absolute size is preserved?
5. Does the cascade gain persist when proposals come from E10 (NWD) instead of E0?
6. What is the median GT box side per class? If MF boxes are ≈20 px and MV/MC ≈60 px, the "40 px" framing hides a 3× spread that matters for the FGVC discussion.
7. Who annotated species labels, at what taxonomic rank, and with what agreement? Were any specimens verified under a microscope?
8. Sec. 6 ¶2 says "no plate appears in two splits". Are plates from the same facility/period nevertheless shared across train and test (site leakage), and could that inflate species accuracy through background memorisation?

## 5. Preliminary Rating
**2** — Reject

## 6. Justification of Rating

The RQ3 thread is the paper's most complete and, in my view, most valuable part: a cleanly selected fine-grained stage that lifts per-species recall on a hard industrial task, with honest cost and limitation reporting (S1–S5). However, in the present draft the central taxonomic claim — that the cascade disproves the irreducibility of the flesh-fly/blow-fly boundary — is contradicted by the paper's own per-species numbers (W1), the class codes are at risk of pointing to the wrong insect (W2), the cascade is not evaluated on the paper's own best detector (W3), and the crop design discards the single most discriminative cue available in a fixed-distance rig without discussion (W4–W5). Sec. 4.3, 5.3, 5.5, 7 and the supplementary are empty, so the per-species table this reviewer would base a positive judgement on is not in the PDF. None of this requires new large experiments — the confusion matrices and per-class numbers already exist — but the claims must be rewritten to match them. I would move to Borderline/Accept if W1–W3 and W8 are fixed and W4 is at least discussed with one ablation.
