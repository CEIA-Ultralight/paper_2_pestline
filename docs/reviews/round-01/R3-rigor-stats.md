## 1. Paper Summary

The paper continues a data-centric pipeline for per-species fly counting on industrial sticky traps (four target species, mean object side 44 px in native 1920×1080 frames). It claims (RQ1) that three failure-targeted acquisitions and a real-instance copy-paste augmentation help more than volume-driven synthetic data; (RQ2) that a hybrid $(1-w)\,\mathrm{CIoU}+w\,\mathrm{NWD}$ similarity placed in both heads of an end-to-end YOLO26 gives +1.4 pp VAL mAP50 over CIoU (0.871 vs 0.857, single seed), with $w{=}1$ collapsing and a GCD variant giving the best mAP50:95; and (RQ3) that a detector→crop→ConvNeXt-T "part attention" classifier cascade raises species recall on a 55-image / 918-instance test set from 75.7 % to 80.1 % at unchanged precision while lowering 7-class mAP50 by 4.7 pp. The evidence offered is: for RQ1 none; for RQ2 one run per variant, selected on a 35-image validation set, with a 3×3-seed campaign announced as running; for RQ3 one classifier seed on a test set that Sec. 6 ¶2 concedes was consulted before, plus a second seed reported as "F1 70.1–72.6 %". Sec. 6 states the correct principles (no interpretation of 1–2 pp differences at $n{=}617/918$, evaluators never mixed, ensemble demoted to exploratory).

## 2. Strengths

**S1 — The limitations section states the right statistical principles and states them early (Sec. 6 ¶2–¶4).** The paper already (i) labels the single-seed status of the detector gain, (ii) reports the second classifier seed and that it fell *below* the first, (iii) declares that with 617/918 species instances "differences of 1–2 pp are within this noise, and we do not interpret them", (iv) declares the test set "consolidated, not blind" and demotes the after-the-fact ensemble to exploratory, and (v) forbids cross-table comparison of the two evaluators, quoting the concrete 0.857 vs 0.812 discrepancy on the same E0 checkpoint. This is more than most CVPR detection papers offer, and this reviewer weighs it strongly in the paper's favour. The problem (W1–W5) is that the abstract, Sec. 1 and the tables do not yet obey these principles.

**S2 — The RQ3 design is inherently paired and therefore cheap to test properly (Tab. 3; `PAPER_FACTS` §3).** The detector, its proposals and the 918 test instances are held fixed; only the label assigned to each matched proposal changes. That makes McNemar's exact test on the discordant pairs (baseline-correct/cascade-wrong vs. the reverse) the exact right test, with no new training. The net gain of +40 correctly localised-and-labelled instances (695→735) is large enough that, even in the least favourable discordance pattern ($c \le 183$, $b = c + 40$), $\chi^2 \ge 40^2/406 \approx 3.9$, i.e. $p \lesssim 0.05$ for the seed-42 classifier. The authors have the per-image predictions on disk (`test-campaign-20260909-v3/full/`) to run this in minutes.

**S3 — The "accuracy up / mAP down" explanation is statistically sound (Sec. 1 P4-3; Sec. 2 ¶3; `CASCADE_RESULTS_20260909.md`).** Re-labelling without re-ranking changes decisions at a threshold but not the ordering of scores, so a threshold-free metric (AP) and a threshold metric (P/R at 0.25) can move in opposite directions. The validation-set confusion counts (496→522 correct of 617 at IoU≥0.5, score≥0.25) support it. This is a useful caution for practitioners who evaluate bolt-on classifiers with COCO AP.

**S4 — Negative results are numerous and most of them are large relative to the known noise (Sec. 1 P4-4; `PAPER_FACTS` §4).** P2 head −5.7 F1, slicing inference −7.0 F1, SAM cleaning ≈−7 pp, 512-px crops −2.9 F1 are 2–3× the observed classifier seed spread; reporting them at all is good practice (Dodge et al. 2019).

**S5 — Every written number matches `PAPER_FACTS.md` numerically** (0.857/0.871/0.864/0.672/0.862/0.626; Tab. 3 all cells; 617/918/2,864/4,197; 70.1–72.6 %; 416.5/22.9 and 463/25 ms; 0.857 vs 0.812). The discrepancies this reviewer found (W1, W2, W6) are between `PAPER_FACTS.md` and the raw artefacts, or of *status*, not of transcription.

## 3. Weaknesses

**Major (must fix before submission)**

1. **The headline RQ2 gain (+1.4 pp, Sec. 0; Sec. 1 P4-2; Sec. 6 ¶3; Tab. 2 caption) is a checkpoint-selection artefact: the E10 number is the maximum over 137 per-epoch validations, not the checkpoint the training actually saved, and under either consistent rule the gain disappears.** From `flydet_runs/E10_yolo26m_nwd/runs/yolo26m.pt/results.csv`: max mAP50 = 0.8706 at epoch 64, but Ultralytics' `best.pt` (fitness $=0.1\,\mathrm{mAP50}+0.9\,\mathrm{mAP50{:}95}$) is epoch 107 with mAP50 = 0.8484, mAP50:95 = 0.6177; the final validation of that `best.pt` in the job-33015 log gives 0.8544. For E0 the two rules coincide (epoch 80, 0.8565, mAP50:95 0.6198), as they do for E11 (ep 87) and E10b (ep 87). So Sec. 1 P4-2 compares E10's *max-over-epochs* against E0's *best.pt*; under a consistent best.pt rule E10 is **−0.8 pp** mAP50 and −0.2 pp mAP50:95 vs. E0; under a consistent max-over-epochs rule the comparison is a max-of-$n$ statistic with $n{=}137$ vs. $n{=}110$ draws. The plateau statistics make the point directly: for epochs ≥ 51, E0 = 0.832 ± 0.010 (n=60), E10 = 0.834 ± 0.013 (n=87), E11 = 0.832 ± 0.014, E10b = 0.831 ± 0.013 — four indistinguishable distributions; the expected maximum of 87 draws from $\mathcal N(0.834, 0.013)$ is ≈ 0.834 + 2.5σ ≈ 0.866, which is what the "best epoch" column reports. **Required:** (a) define the checkpoint-selection rule once in Sec. 5.1 and apply it to every row of Tab. 2 (the defensible choice is `best.pt`, which is also the checkpoint used downstream and released); (b) correct `PAPER_FACTS.md` §1, which currently labels 0.8706 "best epoch"; (c) remove "+1.4 pp" from the abstract, Sec. 1 and Sec. 6 until the multi-seed comparison exists; (d) report the plateau mean ± sd (or last-10-epoch mean) as a secondary, selection-free statistic. Note also that Sec. 1 P4-2 states E12 "collapses … with early stopping at epoch 10": `results.csv` shows 32 epochs, best fitness at epoch 2 (2 + patience 30 = 32) and max mAP50 at epoch 10 — the epoch-10 figure is again the max-over-epochs, and "early stopping at epoch 10" is not what happened.

2. **The announced 3×3-seed campaign (Sec. 0, Sec. 1 P4-2, Sec. 6 ¶3, Tab. 2 caption: "mean±std over 3 seeds") is currently producing exact replicates of seed 0, so its std will be 0.000 and its mean will equal the single-seed number.** In `slurm_logs/flydet-E10y-full_33015.out` the wrapper prints `FLYDET_SEED=1 aplicada` and `FLYDET_SEED=2 aplicada`, but the trainer banner shows `seed=0` for all three runs and `flydet_runs/E10_nwd_seed{0,1}/runs/yolo26m.pt/args.yaml` both contain `seed: 0`; `results.csv` of `E10_nwd_seed0`, `E10_nwd_seed1` and the original `E10_yolo26m_nwd` are identical epoch by epoch (137 epochs, 0.8706 @ 64, 0.8484 @ 107), and the W&B summaries of seeds 0 and 1 report identical mAP50 = 0.85443 and mAP50:95 = 0.61804. The cause is visible in `fly-det/scripts/train_nwd.py` lines 20–28: `init_seeds(int(seed))` is called *before* `model.train()`, and the Ultralytics trainer then calls `init_seeds(self.args.seed + 1 + RANK)` with `args.seed = 0`, overwriting it. The same applies to the E0 seeds 0–2 in the same chain. **Required:** pass `seed=` through the trainer arguments (not via a pre-emptive `init_seeds`), verify in `args.yaml` that the three seeds differ and that `results.csv` files are not identical, and only then fill Tab. 2. Until this is done the paper has **no** measurement of detector seed variance at all — the "several points" figure in Sec. 6 ¶3 comes from the classifier stage, not from the detector.

3. **The RQ3 headline does not replicate on the second seed, and Sec. 6 ¶3 under-reports this (Tab. 3; Sec. 0; Sec. 1 P4-3; Sec. 6 ¶3).** Sec. 6 says the second seed "gave F1 between 70.1 % and 72.6 %, below the reported seed". The reader is not told that the detector-only baseline is 73.2 %: on seed 84 the part-attention cascade (`RELATORIO_TESTE_20260909.md`, Annex A, "F/partes seed 84 / Original") gives P 63.7 / R 77.9 / F1 70.1 — i.e. **−7.2 pp precision, −3.1 pp F1, +2.2 pp recall** relative to YOLO alone, versus +0.2 / +2.1 / +4.4 on seed 42. Three of the four claims in the abstract and Sec. 1 P4-3 ("at fixed precision", "essentially unchanged precision", "F1 from 73.2 % to 75.3 %") therefore hold on one seed and reverse on the other; only the sign of the recall gain is stable, and its magnitude halves. **Required:** (a) Tab. 3 must show both seeds (or mean ± range over the two, with a third seed if at all possible — one classifier training is cheap relative to the detector); (b) the abstract and Sec. 1 must drop "at fixed precision" and the F1 claim, and state the recall gain as a range (+2.2 to +4.4 pp); (c) the per-species claims planned for Sec. 5.3 ("MD 54.3→66.2") must be checked against seed 84 (MD recall 59.1 % on seed 84, Annex A) before being written; (d) the paired McNemar test of S2 should be run for both seeds.

4. **The test set has been used at least 38 times, and Sec. 5.1 plans to describe it as "touched once with the pre-registered champion" (Sec. 5.1 TODO; Sec. 6 ¶2; `RELATORIO_TESTE_20260909.md` Annex A/B).** Annex A lists 38 end-to-end configurations and Annex B 19 classifier configurations evaluated on TEST, including the $w$-free "N/atenção a partes" row that Tab. 3 reports. Sec. 6 ¶2 is honest about this ("consolidated, not blind") but Sec. 5.1 must not claim the opposite, and the paper must say *how many* configurations were evaluated on TEST and that the selection of the part-attention classifier — while made on VAL — was one of ≥ 12 classifier variants (Swin-T, ConvNeXt-T, Focal, ConvNeXt-S, 512-px, SupCon, ArcFace, parts, hires, three combinations, two seeds) whose VAL differences were of the same order as the VAL noise floor (see W5). Under a Holm correction over 12 VAL comparisons, a single-seed difference of 1–2 pp at $n{=}617$ cannot be declared significant. The RQ2 detector selection (E10 vs E11, E10b, E14, E7, TTA/WBF) has the same structure and the same remedy: state the number of variants compared, the selection rule, and report the champion's TEST number *once*, with a bootstrap CI, after the seeds are fixed (W2). RQ1 (Tab. 1) has not touched TEST and is the one place where a genuinely blind test number is still possible; the authors should protect it.

5. **No confidence intervals or minimum detectable differences are given although the sample sizes permit exact statements (Sec. 6 ¶3; Tab. 2; Tab. 3).** At $n{=}918$ and $p \approx 0.78$ the 95 % Wald half-width of a single recall/precision is ≈ 2.7 pp; the unpaired minimum detectable difference between two such proportions (α = 0.05, power 0.8) is ≈ 5.4 pp, and ≈ 6.4 pp at $n{=}617$. The paper's "1–2 pp is noise" statement is therefore *too optimistic* for unpaired comparisons (different classifiers, different seeds) and *too pessimistic* for the paired one (same detector, relabelled, W-S2). For the detector, mAP50 on 35 validation images has an image-level bootstrap CI that the authors can compute from saved predictions; the epoch-to-epoch sd on the plateau (≈ 0.010–0.013, W1) is a usable proxy for the evaluation noise floor and already exceeds most of the reported inter-variant differences (E10−E11 = 0.6 pp, E10−E10b = 0.9 pp, GCD mAP50:95 − E0 = 0.6 pp). **Required:** give the CI or MDD once in Sec. 5.1, add 95 % bootstrap CIs (over images for mAP, over instances or images for P/R) to Tab. 2 and Tab. 3, and apply the same threshold to negative results (W7).

6. **The "part attention" component has no measured effect over a plain cross-entropy ConvNeXt-T, yet it is named as the mechanism (Sec. 0; Sec. 1 P4-3; Sec. 2 ¶3 "part attention transfers to this scale").** Annex A, seed 42, "Original" score policy: ConvNeXt-T (CE) P 71.7 / R 80.1 / F1 75.7; re-trained CE baseline "N/baseline" 70.9 / 79.5 / 75.0; part attention 71.2 / 80.1 / 75.4. Recall is identical to the decimal (80.07 %) and F1 differs by 0.3 pp — far inside the seed spread (W3). The classifier was legitimately selected on VAL, but the paper must (a) include the plain-CE cascade as a row in Tab. 3 so that the reader sees the gain comes from *cascading*, not from *part attention*; (b) reword Sec. 2 ¶3 to "did not hurt" rather than "transfers"; (c) not describe ArcFace/SupCon as "do not improve" with more certainty than the parts head is credited — all three are within noise of each other.

## 5. Preliminary Rating

**2** — Reject (provisional; the manuscript is incomplete and the two quantitative headline claims do not currently survive a consistent protocol)

## 6. Justification of Rating

The written sections set an unusually high standard of candour (S1) and the RQ3 mechanism is sound (S3), so this reviewer's concern is not with the authors' intent but with what the present evidence supports. On the detector side, the +1.4 pp gain is produced by comparing a max-over-137-epochs statistic for E10 against a fitness-selected checkpoint for E0; the saved E10 checkpoint is below E0, and the plateau distributions of all four variants are indistinguishable (W1). The multi-seed campaign that the abstract, Sec. 1, Sec. 6 and Tab. 2 rely on is, at the time of review, training the same seed three times (W2), so no detector variance has been measured. On the cascade side, the recall gain is directionally stable across two classifier seeds but the precision and F1 claims reverse sign on the second seed, which Sec. 6 mentions only as a range without noting it falls below the detector-only baseline (W3); and the "part attention" mechanism is indistinguishable from a plain ConvNeXt-T (W6). The test set has been consulted dozens of times (W4), and no CIs are given although $n$ is known (W5). All of these are fixable without new large-scale compute: one training-script fix and the already-budgeted 3×3 rerun, one extra classifier seed, a McNemar test on saved predictions, bootstrap CIs, and rewriting the abstract/Sec. 1 to the standard Sec. 6 already sets. If the corrected seeds still show E10 ≥ E0 and the cascade recall gain holds across ≥ 3 classifier seeds with a paired test, the paper's honesty and its methodological observations would make it a solid Borderline-to-Accept. As it stands the two numerical claims are not evidence.

## 7. Confidence

**5** — The two major findings were verified directly on the raw training logs, `args.yaml` and `results.csv` files rather than inferred from the text; the statistical arguments (max-of-$n$ bias, MDD at the stated $n$, paired vs. unpaired design) are standard. The only uncertainty concerns which of the two Ultralytics validation passes the authors intend as canonical (Minor 2), which does not affect the conclusions.
