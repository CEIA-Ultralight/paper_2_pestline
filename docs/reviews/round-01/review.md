# Review — VISAPP 2027 (Regular paper)

**Paper:** "Decoupling Detection from Identification for Species-Level Fly Monitoring on Industrial Sticky Traps"
**Material reviewed:** the 16-page PDF (read via per-page text and page images supplied by the coordinator; all 16 pages incl. references).
**Form:** Criteria (Relevance, Originality, Technical Quality, Significance, Presentation) and closed questions are official VISAPP; the numeric 1-5 scale and the section layout are inferred (PRIMORIS form not public).

## 1. Paper Summary
The paper studies species-level detection of flies on industrial sticky traps (1920x1080 images, mean object about 44 px, 7 classes). It frames three "levers" for a limit that a prior study called irreducible. (RQ1) Data: a ladder of five cumulative training sets of a YOLO26m detector raises species accuracy from 56.1% to 82.2% (3 seeds), mainly on objects below 32 px and on insect-only plates. (RQ2) Identification: a detect-then-classify cascade (five classifier backbones, three detectors) never beats the single-stage detector (macro-F1 change -1.1 to -5.1 pp). Ground-truth-crop accuracy saturates near 83% for all backbones, which the authors read as an information limit on the MD/MV/MC species. (RQ3) Scale: NWD, RFLA, GCD, Wise-IoU, P2 head, 2560 px input, SAHI, TTA and WBF give no gain, and NWD+RFLA is 8.8 pp worse.

## 2. Strengths
- Relevant, real-world application (field images at production resolution, not laboratory close-ups), and a clearly stated, honest "negative results" framing.
- Good statistical hygiene for this field: three seeds, paired bootstrap over images, Holm correction, explicit caveats (McNemar independence, "interval including zero is not equivalence", NWD+RFLA instability reported rather than hidden).
- The ground-truth-crop oracle plus a localized/not-localized/wrong-species decomposition (Sec. 4.4, 5.2) is a useful way to separate detection from identification. The background-class, late-fusion and abstention checks pre-empt obvious objections.
- Candour about protocol (checkpoint selected on VAL, test split "consulted" earlier, ensemble reported as exploratory).

## 3. Weaknesses

### Fatal (for the submission as it stands)
- **F1. The manuscript is not a finished paper (whole document, e.g. pp. 1-3, 5-7, 11, 13).** It contains many unresolved `[TODO ...]` blocks: missing class table and dataset statistics (Sec. 3.3), a missing Figure (cascade diagram, Sec. 4.2), a missing confusion-matrix figure (Sec. 5.4), missing dataset comparison (Sec. 3.4), "[TODO: Decide public release]" inside contribution 5, undefined GCD citation, and acknowledgements. It also contains internal notes addressed to the authors ("AUTHOR DECISION", "flagged in internal review", file names such as `rq1_versions_summary.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `PAPER_FACTS`, `v9_*.csv`). Sec. 3 (the dataset section, the basis of RQ1) is essentially a list of TODOs. A reviewer cannot verify the data protocol. Would change my view: a complete Sec. 3 with class definitions, split procedure and statistics.
- **F2. Length and format (pp. 1-16).** The PDF is 16 pages against the 12-page limit for a Full paper including references. Even discounting the TODO text, the body is dense and the reference list is long. This may prevent the paper from going to review.
- **F3. Anonymity (Sec. 1, 2.4, 3.1).** The paper is positioned as the "model-centric counterpart" of "(Queiroz et al., 2026)", which it "extends" and uses the same dataset lineage and SOP. Although cited in third person, the continuity (same dataset, "that study concluded", "inherited SOP", "partner identity (double-blind)" TODO) makes the authorship near-obvious. Also the acknowledgement section is a TODO and AI-disclosure is not yet filled.

### Major
- **M1. The data-ladder claim is confounded (Sec. 5.1, Table 3).** Quantity, quality, annotation protocol, species-purity, crowding, background (white paper, about 42% of L2+ORG) and copy-paste all change together (the authors admit this). The "equal-size subsample control was not run" (Sec. 5.1, Sec. 6 limitations). The headline "56% to 82% from targeted collections" is therefore really "more data (103 to 1,738 images, 4.4k to 88k boxes) gives better detection". Contribution 5 ("targeted physical collection, a strategy absent from the literature") is not isolated by any experiment against, e.g., randomly collected plates of equal size. Also L3 removes the white-paper photographs and adds copy-paste simultaneously (non-monotone design). Would change my view: an equal-size control or at least a per-collection leave-one-out.
- **M2. Hypothesis H2 and its framing (Sec. 5.1).** H2 ("identification does not move") is contradicted by the authors' own results (MV->MC 14.8 to 5.4%, MF to non-species 46 to 9%), and they then say "part of the confusion is a data effect". This partly undermines the paper's own thesis ("identification does not move with architecture" is a different claim than the Abstract's wording that reads as data not helping identification). Confusion rates have no confidence intervals (TODO admitted), seeds pooled, and the "MC->MV stays near 12%" claim cannot be distinguished from noise. The tension between RQ1 and RQ2 is noted in a TODO but not resolved.
- **M3. The "information-limited ceiling" conclusion is not supported (Sec. 5.2, Conclusion).** The abstract states that the oracle saturation locates the limit "in the information available ... rather than in the architecture". Evidence: five backbones at 82.7-83.8% (same label set, same crops, same train data), a flat learning curve with one seed and three points (83.0/81.7/82.6), confident-learning flags at 3.7% with no expert review, and 4.9% errors shared by all. The authors themselves say "does not prove it". Alternative explanations are not tested: label noise in the species-pure-plate labelling scheme (labels come from a handwritten plate label, so species-purity is by plate, not per specimen; contamination of plates is not discussed), crop context size (75% padding, 384 px resize of 44 px objects, i.e. 8x upsampling), training with 88k boxes mostly INS/NOISE, no expert re-annotation, no multi-view/higher-resolution capture. The practical recommendation "treat it as a labeling and imaging problem" is not derived from any experiment. Also, the claim contradicts Sec. 5.1 where more/better data lowered the confusion.
- **M4. Cascade comparison is weaker than "15 configurations" suggests (Sec. 4.2, 5.2).** The 15 configurations are 5 backbones x 3 detectors, but only E0 has three seeds; YOLO26l has one seed, and four of five backbones have a single classifier seed (only CE and parts are replicated). The nine E0 pairs are not independent. Most per-configuration CIs include zero (e.g. -1.4 [-3.7,+1.3]), and the pairwise McNemar p-values range from 0.005 to 0.51. "Same sign in all 15" is the main argument, but the 15 are strongly correlated (same test images, shared detectors). The cascade also uses a confidence floor of 0.001 with up to 300 crops per image, a 7-way classifier that can relabel to NOISE/INS, and a label-only fusion; the best alternative designs (true class-score fusion, selective reclassification) are either approximated or "not run" or not reported (TODO: outcome of policy study missing). So "cascade does not help" is shown for one specific, not necessarily well-tuned, design, yet the title and abstract generalise it.
- **M5. Test-set protocol and VAL reliance (Sec. 4.4).** VAL has 52 images and picks best.pt, classifier head ("parts" chosen on VAL), and policies. The test split (73 images) "was consulted in earlier campaigns of this project", an ensemble was assembled after seeing test results, and the authors admit there may be no hold-out. With 73 images (7 crowded, 6 INS-only plates) and 1,210 instances from a single facility, results are fragile; all RQ conclusions rest on one split. Plate-level or period-level split is a TODO, so leakage between plates (same species-pure session, same glue background; 4.7% of test crops have similarity >=0.98 to a training crop) cannot be excluded. Copy-paste contributes 607 training images; whether pasted crops come from test-like plates is not stated.
- **M6. RQ3 conclusions overreach (Sec. 5.3, Table 4/5, Abstract).** (a) "None of eight variants beats the control": intervals are about +/-1.7 pp, so differences are unresolved, not absent; the authors say so for GCD/WIoU/NWD-assigner but the abstract states "no measurable gain". (b) NWD+RFLA's -8.8 pp is driven by one early-stopped and one diverged run (nan), not retried; this is an implementation/instability finding rather than evidence about transfer. (c) The whole campaign is run on a different GPU (L40S, batch size and numerics may differ) and the NWD constant C=44 px was measured on an earlier data version. (d) Only one YOLO family/size (YOLO26m, one seed for YOLO11m), no anchor-based or two-stage baseline on which NWD/RFLA were originally shown to work, so the explanation "why aerial recipes do not transfer" (Sec. 5.3) is speculation, as the authors admit ("not tested causes"). (e) The SAHI test uses a model trained at 1920 but sliced at 512 tiles and the "whole frame resized to 512" comparator is acknowledged as unfair; no tile-trained model, so SAHI is not fairly evaluated. The intro says sliced inference "remains inconclusive" while the conclusion lists it among methods that bring no gain.
- **M7. Numerical/definition inconsistencies (Sec. 4.4, 5, Tables).** (i) Sec. 4.4 says aggregate metrics are micro-averaged "over the 918 test instances" and that species instances lie "in 51 of the 55 test images", while everywhere else the test has 73 images and 1,210 species instances (MD 224 + MV 302 + MC 268 + MF 416 = 1,210). The 918/55 numbers seem to come from another split version; the statistical tests are described with them. (ii) Species accuracy for E0 is 82.2% (Table 3, L3 "runs are the E0 seeds") but 81.7% in Table 4 (separate L40S E0) and "macro-F1 79.5%" in 5.2; the three protocols (accuracy=micro recall, macro-F1, mAP@50 from a different evaluator at conf 0.0 in Table 5 where E0 mAP@50 is 84.2) are hard to reconcile for readers. Recall <32 px is 80.5% in Table 3 but 79.4% in Table 4 for the same nominal model. (iii) The "<32 px recall nearly doubles" in the abstract refers to L0 (103 images) vs L3. (iv) MD->MV +MD->non-species+missed should reconcile with MD correct 64.9% in Table 6, but the missed share is not shown, so the table cannot be checked. (v) "Species accuracy" is defined as recall, which ignores false positives; precision is hardly reported in the main text although the Conventions say it is primary.
- **M8. Novelty and positioning (Sec. 1, 2).** The contributions are empirical negative results with standard components (YOLO26, ConvNeXt, DINOv2/3, BioCLIP 2, NWD, SAHI). The only new element is the dataset, whose release is undecided (TODO in contribution 5), so it cannot be counted as a contribution. The claim "no sticky-trap study to date reports multi-seed evaluation" and "no precedent in the insect-monitoring literature" for physical targeted collection are strong, unverifiable absolute claims. The comparison with the prior study's "irreducible" claim is cast as refuting it, but the prior data are different (1280x720, 6 classes, different acquisition) so the two are not comparable.

### Minor
- Only YOLO-family and RT-DETR-l detectors; no recent small-object-specific detector or fine-grained detection method outside the two-stage design (e.g., a single-stage detector with an auxiliary fine-grained head) as a competitive baseline. Not verifiable whether stronger baselines would change RQ2.
- Class acronym collision with the prior paper (MF flesh fly vs fruit fly) is flagged in a TODO and the "housefly/blowfly/flesh-fly family" statement in the Abstract/Conclusions depends on it; species names are never given in full in the body, so a reader cannot know what MD, MV, MC, MF mean. This is essential for a species-level paper.
- Cost comparison (30-67x) is indicative only (shared node, one timing per image, FP32); a cascade batched on 300 crops per image at conf 0.001 is an artificially expensive design; thresholding proposals would lower it.
- Table 2 and 3 are dense; Fig. 1 confusion matrices are too small to read at one-column width and rely on small numbers (pooled seeds, no CIs).
- Confident-learning discussion (3.7% suspect) is used as evidence both for and against label noise.
- Related work is long and partly generic (Sec. 2.3 contains a paragraph of loosely related items: confident learning, statistical protocol); some cited works are preprints or 2026 items that I cannot verify.
- Reproducibility: code and data release are undecided; training details are good, but total GPU-hours and wall clock are TODO.
- The English is generally good, but sentences are very long and the text mixes protocol-level detail with results; the text uses "we" for data collection that is not described.

## 4. Questions for the authors
1. What is the correct size of the test set used for statistics (73 images/1,210 instances vs 55 images/918 instances in Sec. 4.4)? Which numbers in the paper use which?
2. Was the split made by plate, collection session or period? Can specimens/glue backgrounds from the same plate appear in train and test? What is the source of the 4.7% near-duplicate test crops?
3. Can you provide an equal-size random-subsample control (or per-collection leave-one-out) for the ladder so that "targeted" can be separated from "more"?
4. What is the species-purity error rate (a mislabelled or contaminated plate)? Has any expert verified a sample of the MD/MV/MC test instances? Without it the "information limit" and the "labelling problem" readings are indistinguishable.
5. Does the cascade still lose if the classifier is trained on detector-proposed crops (matching the test distribution), with a thresholded number of proposals, or with real detector class scores fused?
6. Were the NWD+RFLA divergences caused by the implementation (e.g., C, assigner) rather than by the method? Why not retry with other seeds?
7. Will the dataset be released? If not, which contribution remains?
8. How different would conclusions be on a second, independent facility or trap colour?

## 6. Overall rating and justification
**Overall: 2 (Reject)** in its current state. The research questions are interesting and the negative-results stance is honest and, once the manuscript is completed and tightened, could be a solid applied contribution (I would then lean to Borderline/Accept). Right now the paper cannot be evaluated as submitted: the dataset section is missing, figures are missing, the page limit is exceeded by 4 pages, and several headline claims (targeted collection as the cause of the gain; information-limited ceiling; no transfer of tiny-object methods) are stronger than the evidence. What would move me to Accept: a complete Sec. 3 with split protocol and class definitions, an equal-size data control, a resolved count/metric inconsistency, an expert check on a sample of confusable labels, and toned-down claims in the Abstract/Title. Question that decides the borderline case: is there a plate-level split with no leakage, and can the authors show the ladder gain is not just dataset size?

## 8. Closed questions
- The abstract and introduction are adequate? **Partial.** Clear, but the abstract overstates ("locating the residual limit in the information available", "no measurable gain"); the intro is long and contribution 5 contains a TODO.
- More experimental results are needed? **Yes.** Equal-size data control, independent test/hold-out, expert labelling check, a better-tuned cascade variant, tile-trained SAHI, retried NWD+RFLA.
- A comparative evaluation is needed? **Partial.** Cascade vs single-stage is done; missing stronger baselines for tiny/fine-grained detection and (for RQ3) an anchor-based/two-stage detector where NWD/RFLA are known to help.
- The paper needs a more critical discussion? **Yes.** The RQ1/RQ2 tension, label-noise alternatives, generality beyond one facility.
- The figures are adequate? **No.** Only one figure (small confusion matrices); the cascade diagram and the confusion-matrix figure are missing; no qualitative examples; tables are dense.
- The conclusions and future work are convincing? **Partial.** Conclusions on data and the cascade are partly supported; the "labeling and imaging problem" advice and the transfer explanations are not.
- The references are up-to-date and appropriate? **Mostly yes**, but several 2026 preprints cannot be verified by me and some items are tangential (not verifiable from the manuscript).
- The formatting needs adjustment? **Yes.** 16 pages vs the 12-page limit; TODO blocks and internal notes; tables overflow columns.
- The English needs improvement? **Partial.** Understandable, but very long, compressed sentences; needs editing for concision.

## 9. Comments to the authors
The negative-result approach is welcome, and the care with statistics is above the norm. To make it publishable: finish the dataset section (class names in full, split by plate/period, statistics, release decision); fix the 918/55 versus 1,210/73 inconsistency; reduce the paper to 12 pages; remove all internal notes; add the missing figures; and align the title/abstract with what was shown (a single cascade design, one facility, one split). Add an equal-size data control; otherwise reframe the first contribution as "more and better curated data helps", not as "targeted collection". Report confusion CIs. For RQ3, retry the diverged NWD+RFLA runs and either test the stated explanations (e.g., analyse the NWD/IoU distribution, add an anchor-based baseline) or label them clearly as hypotheses. Consider reporting precision/F1 per species in a main-text table (the conventions say per-species recall/precision are primary), since species accuracy as defined equals recall.

## 10. Confidential comments to the Program Chair
No plagiarism concern. Anonymity is at risk: the paper explicitly extends a prior study by the same group (cited as Queiroz et al., 2026) and the text and TODOs refer to a partner identity, internal documents and file names; an AI-use disclosure is a TODO. I would either desk-reject on format/completeness grounds or, if the chairs prefer a review, I recommend Reject with encouragement to resubmit a complete version.
