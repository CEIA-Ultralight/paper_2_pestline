Conference: VISAPP 2027 (regular full paper, double-blind)
Paper: "Decoupling Detection from Identification for Species-Level Fly Monitoring on Industrial Sticky Traps"
Profile: partially [inferido] (numeric scale, exact PRIMORIS layout, template, section ordering of the form). Criteria names and closed questions are [oficial].
Read: all 12 pages of the PDF (text and rendered pages; figure 1 read from its text extraction, so only its numbers, not its visual quality, were verified).

## 1. Paper Summary
The paper studies species-level detection of four fly species (MD, MV, MC, MF; plus INS/NOISE/MAR) on 1920x1080 industrial sticky-trap photographs (mean object about 44 px). It asks three questions on the DS-F2 dataset using YOLO26m. RQ1: a ladder of five cumulative training sets (103 to 1,738 images) raises species accuracy from 56.1% to 82.2% (3 seeds); a partial equal-size control (random subsets of the final set) matches the 244- and 869-image rungs, so the rise up to there is mostly quantity. RQ2: a frozen-detector, detect-then-classify cascade (five classifiers, four backbones, three detectors) never beats the detector alone (macro-F1 -1.1 to -5.1 pp), and ground-truth-crop accuracy saturates near 83%. RQ3: eight tiny-object variants from the aerial literature (NWD, RFLA, GCD, Wise-IoU v3, P2, 2560 px, YOLO11m) give no detectable gain, and NWD+RFLA is 8.8 pp worse. Code will be released; images will not.

## 2. Strengths
- Candid, well-calibrated writing. Most claims are hedged, VAL vs TEST is separated, and the lack of a held-out set is stated openly (Sec. 4.4, Limitations).
- Task of real practical interest (species-level reading of field traps at native resolution). Negative results with a same-campaign control and paired bootstrap are useful to practitioners.
- Per-species, instance-level primary metrics with a fixed operating point are more meaningful than mAP for this application. The oracle (ground-truth crops) cleanly separates localization from identification.
- The authors themselves ran the equal-size control that undermines their data narrative, and report it prominently.
- Multi-seed reporting with deltas paired to the same-seed control (Tables 1-2).

## 3. Weaknesses

### Fatal
None that I would call fatal on its own. However, the combination of M1, M2 and M3 below leaves the headline conclusions weakly supported (see rating).

### Major
M1. Evaluation set is too small and possibly leaky, and no clean held-out exists. TEST is 73 images (1,210 instances; 7 crowded and 6 insect-only plates); VAL is 52 images (Sec. 3.3, 4.4). The dataset has no plate identifier, so images of the same plate may sit on both sides of a split (Sec. 3.3, Limitations); 4.7% of TEST crops are near-duplicates (thumbnail similarity >=0.98) of training crops (Sec. 5.2). Instances within an image are strongly correlated, and 1,210 instances come from only 73 images. TEST was also "consulted in earlier campaigns" (Sec. 4.4), and an earlier ensemble was built after seeing test results. Every absolute number (82%, 83% oracle ceiling) and most deltas are therefore of uncertain validity. What would change my view: a plate/session-grouped split, or a fresh set of plates collected after the protocol was frozen.

M2. RQ1 does not establish what the paper's framing suggests, and the framing is internally inconsistent. (a) The ladder confounds quantity, quality, annotation version, domain and class balance (acknowledged), and the control is only partial: no control at 103 images, 2 seeds at 869, and the 244-image subsamples range 0.69-0.84 (std 0.077), so the control has almost no power to detect a targeting effect of the size of interest. "No measurable advantage" is therefore absence of evidence, not evidence of absence, yet the abstract and conclusion state "mostly quantity". (b) The localized gains (small objects, insect-only plates, crowded plates) are exactly what the targeted collections were meant to fix, and the paper itself admits these were not tested against the control (Sec. 5.1 "Threats"). The insect-only result (75.7 -> 0 false positives) is unsurprising: L0 and L1 contain essentially no INS-only plates (8 images appear in v7), so it is a trivially expected effect, not a finding. (c) Introduction says "no equal-size control was run" (p. 2) while Contribution 1 and the abstract rely on one. This is a visible contradiction. (d) Hypothesis H2 ("identification does not move") is refuted by the paper's own Table 1/Fig. 1 (MV->MC 14.8 -> 5.4, MC->MV 18.5 -> 11.7), but the text says "only partly supported", and the pooled confusion rates have no intervals. The ladder is also not a test of the prior paper's "irreducible" claim, since that data differs (Sec. 2.4 concedes "indicative, not like-for-like"), so the framing in the abstract and intro ("deemed irreducible") overstates what is refuted.

M3. The cascade negative result is a statement about one weak recipe, but is phrased broadly (title, abstract, RQ2 "Does a detect-then-classify cascade improve..."). Design choices that plausibly handicap the cascade: frozen detector with a 0.001 confidence floor sending up to 300 proposals per image to the classifier; the classifier is trained on annotated (clean) boxes but applied to noisy proposals; 75% margin per side on a 44 px object (so a crop of about 110 px upsampled to 384, dominated by background and neighbours in crowded plates); a 7-way head, so the classifier replaces detector information on INS/NOISE/MAR (this is what produces the "MD -> non-species +8.0 pp" in Sec. 5.4); label-only fusion; and a classifier that never sees the detector score. The true-score late fusion was "not run", and the approximate fusion is admitted to be an approximation. No variant is tried where the classifier only re-labels among the four species (restricting to species-vs-species given detector species-class), no crop-margin ablation, no detector-trained-with-hard-negatives classifier, no joint training. "Does not improve in any of 15 configurations" counts correlated configurations (same detector seeds, same crops, same recipe) as if independent evidence; DINOv2/DINOv3/BioCLIP are single-seed and YOLO26l is single-seed. The cascade also compares F1 of two systems whose difference is mostly in how non-species classes are handled, not in species discrimination. The claim of "limited information in the images" is not supported: the oracle ceiling does not distinguish ambiguity from label noise (confident learning flags only 3.7%, but its flags are the same confused pairs by construction), crop recipe, or 44 px resolution. Yet the paper also never examines the simplest explanation, label noise from a handwritten-label, plate-level annotation scheme (species is assigned by the plate label, not by expert per-specimen inspection - Sec. 3.2). If species labels are inherited from the plate, MD/MV/MC "errors" at 83% accuracy may be mostly mislabeled contaminants. This is the single most important unexamined threat and is relegated to future work.

M4. The RQ3 conclusions are under-powered and the causal language is not backed by evidence. Three seeds, a 73-image test set, CIs of about +-1.7 pp, and seed std up to 3.4-7.5 pp. The paper acknowledges "no difference detected, not equivalence", but the title of contribution 4 and the practitioner message ("what does not transfer") read as stronger. (a) Default hyperparameters only, with NWD constant C=44 px estimated from a different data version, and with no tuning for any variant, while the control got the Ultralytics defaults that were presumably developed on COCO-like data; fairness of "no tuning for all" is arguable. (b) NWD+RFLA is attributed to "unstable seeds" (one stopped early at epoch 40, one diverged with nan): this is an implementation/training failure, not evidence about the method, and it should not be reported as a variant that is "8.8 points worse" in the abstract. The paper chose "did not retry", which is reasonable for honesty but means this row is uninformative. (c) The "why it does not transfer" paragraph (stride-8 cells, task-aligned assigner, native scale) is plausible but untested; there is no experiment (e.g., on downscaled input where NWD should help, or on an anchor-based detector) that would validate the explanation, which would have been the actual contribution. (d) RQ3 ran on a different GPU (L40S) with a different E0 than RQ1/RQ2 (81.7 vs 82.2 for the same nominal configuration; recall <32 px 79.4 vs 80.5), so results cannot be pooled and nondeterminism across hardware is not quantified. (e) The sliced-inference comparison is mixed in a different evaluator (mAP@50 at confidence 0.0, all classes), cannot be compared to the species numbers, uses a model not trained on tiles ("A tile-trained model was not run"), so it is a straw man; the finding that SAHI applied to a 1920-trained model with 512-px tiles loses is expected.

M5. Novelty and contribution are thin for the venue. The method contributions are none (existing YOLO26, ConvNeXt/DINO backbones, NWD/RFLA taken off the shelf); the dataset is private ("the images will not [be released]"), so neither data nor ladder can be reproduced or reused, and the paper's central object (DS-F2) is not a contribution that the community can verify. The surviving contribution is an empirical study, but its findings are largely "adding data helps up to a point, a naive cascade does not help, off-the-shelf small-object tricks do not help": each is individually weak (see M1-M4). The code release promise helps but cannot reproduce results without images or at least model weights/prediction files. At minimum, release per-image predictions, annotation files for TEST (or anonymized crops), and the exact splits.

M6. Inconsistencies and counting that weaken trust (verified in the PDF).
- Intro p. 2: "no equal-size control was run" vs Contribution 1/abstract: equal-size control shows the rise "is mostly quantity".
- Contribution 2 says "five classifier backbones and three detectors"; abstract and Sec. 1 say "five classifiers (four backbones)"; Sec. 4.2 lists CE, parts, ConvNeXt-T, DINOv2, DINOv3, BioCLIP 2, and says ArcFace and SupCon were trained but "not reported". Reporting that two heads were trained once and silently dropped is a selective-reporting concern; report them in an appendix.
- Sec. 4.2/4.4: "the choice of the 'parts' head as the main classifier" was made on VAL, but Sec. 4.2 calls ConvNeXt-T CE the main classifier and the control; Sec. 5.2 reports both. Which is the primary comparison? The "15 configurations" count mixes single-seed and 3-seed items.
- "15 configurations" (5 classifiers x 3 detectors) vs "15 classifiers" in "4.9% are wrong for all fifteen classifiers" (p. 2, p. 8): two different 15s. The latter needs to be defined (which 15?) since only five classifier types are listed.
- Sec. 5.2: "every one of the five stage-2 backbones lowers it", but 4 of 5 CIs on E0 include zero; only CE reaches significance (p_perm = 0.013), before any multiple-comparison correction across the 15 configurations. "Sign is the same in all 5x3 configurations" is a sign test over correlated runs.
- MD recall "0.64-0.69 from L1 on" and the MD->MV 26.3% error: the species most important for sanitary monitoring (housefly) is the worst, but is not analysed further.
- Abstract: "species accuracy ... rises from 56% to 82%", while Sec. 5.1 defines accuracy as localized and correctly named, which equals micro recall (Sec. 5.3). "Accuracy" is a misnomer; it is recall at fixed threshold. Reporting precision per species (the stated primary metrics: "recall, precision, F1") is absent from Tables 1-2, although precision is what the insect-only false-positive result is about.

### Minor
m1. Single operating point (score 0.25, IoU 0.5) chosen "before test evaluation" but never justified or varied; no PR curves or sensitivity of conclusions (especially the cascade, whose score distribution differs from the detector's) to the threshold. The cascade keeps the detector score, so a classifier trained with label smoothing and a very different calibration may be penalized at a fixed threshold.
m2. McNemar tests on instances and image-level bootstrap with 73 images; tests over 36 pairs with Holm, but the abstract reports no corrected result. Cluster at the plate level would be better than the image level.
m3. Fig. 1 (confusion matrices) is placed mid-page with tiny cells and pooled seeds; no per-seed spread. The grey-scale colour bar and text at one-column width look hard to read (not verifiable beyond text extraction). Tables 1-2 are dense; Table 1 mixes counts in thousands and means without units in all rows.
m4. The mean object size reported as sqrt(wh) ~44 px with "a large share" below 32 px: give the size distribution (Table 2 implies 613/1,210 = 51% below 32 px). Show the figure of the distribution in the dataset section.
m5. Sec. 3.2 lists collection properties ("verified in four ways") but several are descriptions of the lab protocol that a reader cannot check; the species identity rests on handwritten plate labels (see M3): state the labelling procedure, annotators, inter-annotator agreement, and who verified the species (entomologist?). The 62 re-annotated images between v6.1.1 and v7.1.0 are mentioned only in passing.
m6. Related work: the "we did not find in the literature we reviewed" statements (physical targeted collection; multi-seed statistics in sticky-trap studies) are weak novelty claims. Sec. 2.3 presents the cascade-vs-single-stage comparison as missing from the literature, but there is substantial literature on two-stage fine-grained detection for small objects (e.g., crop re-classification in medical/aerial imaging) not discussed. Brock et al. (2026) is an arXiv preprint cited for a "recent challenge entry"; the arXiv id format and the single non-peer-reviewed source should be checked. GCD is cited only as "arXiv:2510.27649" in the text, not in the reference list style used elsewhere. The citation to Du (2025) for the sign-flip permutation test and to Northcutt et al. (2021a/b) are fine in principle but the claim that 3.7% suspect labels is "in the range reported for curated benchmarks" is not an argument for the dataset's label quality.
m7. Compute: epochs, GPU-hours, and per-run time are not reported; the cost comparison (30-67x latency) is "indicative only" because of node sharing. FP32 on B200 for latency is not a deployment-relevant setting; no edge or CPU number although future work cites edge deployment.
m8. Writing: the paper hedges heavily and repeatedly restates the same limitation (intro, Sec. 5.2, Limitations), consuming space that could host the missing analyses. Some sentences are very long. The abstract is overloaded (about 300 words with many numbers and clauses) and hard to read. The conclusion's practitioner recommendation ("collecting more annotated plates matters more than model complexity") is not supported by a comparison of effort or a learning curve beyond 1,738 images; the paper itself says the last steps give at most +1-2 pp (L2 -> L3), i.e., a plateau.
m9. Anonymity: mostly fine. The prior paper is cited in the third person ("Anonymous, 2026") but the reference title ("A data-centric pipeline for microscale insect detection in industrial sticky traps") and the intro closely paraphrase the earlier work and lean on its numbers (0.476 -> 0.608 mAP, class count, v4 data); the text "this exact setting" and "same dataset lineage" make authorship of the earlier paper evident to anyone who knows it. The portuguese class names (mosca doméstica, etc.) are harmless. Acknowledgements are anonymized. The paper does not contain an AI-use declaration; VISAPP requires one if text is AI-generated. Flag to chair if relevant.
m10. Possible overlap with the prior submission (same dataset lineage, same 6-7 class problem, MC/MV confusion narrative): the paper states that no text/figures are reused, but the extent of overlap should be explicitly bounded for the chair (see confidential comments).
m11. Page budget: 12 pages including references, as required for full papers; the character count was not verifiable from the PDF.

## 4. Questions for the authors
1. How were species labels assigned per box: by the handwritten plate label (species-pure plates) or by expert inspection of each specimen? If the former, what fraction of "MC->MV" and "MD->MV" errors are contamination of the plate by other species, and have you looked at a random sample of 50 confident errors with an entomologist?
2. Can you provide a plate- or session-grouped split (or at least plate IDs recovered from capture timestamps/grid position) and re-run the main comparisons? What is the effect on the 4.7% near-duplicate crops if removed from TEST?
3. Why was the classifier trained on annotated boxes only and not on detector proposals (hard negatives, jittered boxes)? What happens to the cascade if the stage-2 classifier is restricted to the four species and only re-labels boxes the detector already calls a species? What happens with a 25% or 0% margin?
4. What is the power of the equal-size control: with std 0.077 over 244-image subsamples, what targeting effect could be detected? Why not run the missing control at 103 and a third seed at 869, given that it is the pivot of RQ1?
5. Why were ArcFace and SupCon omitted? What were their results?
6. Was the NWD+RFLA divergence caused by an implementation issue (loss scaling, learning rate)? Would a retry with a different seed change the story?
7. Is "species accuracy" ever different from micro recall at 0.25/IoU 0.5? If not, why use two names? Where is per-species precision?
8. Which test would convince you the aerial recipes help in this regime (e.g., downscaled images, anchor-based baseline)? Why not include one to validate the proposed explanation?

## 6. Overall rating and justification
Overall: 2 (Reject), at the upper end of that band (2+). The paper is honest and carefully hedged, and negative results have value. But the evidence base (73 test images, possible plate-level leakage, a private dataset, partial controls, three seeds) does not support the conclusions that the abstract, title and practitioner message draw, and the two most informative experiments (the equal-size control and a stronger cascade) are either incomplete or not run. The decisive issues are M1-M3. I would move to Borderline if the authors, in a rebuttal or revision, (i) provide a plate-grouped split or plate-level analysis and rerun the main deltas, (ii) clarify how species labels were produced and audit a sample of the confident errors with an expert, and (iii) complete the equal-size control (103 images, a third seed at 869) with the localized metrics, and soften the "mostly quantity" and "limited information in the images" claims accordingly.

## 8. Closed questions
- The abstract and introduction are adequate? Partial. The abstract is overloaded; the intro contradicts the contributions on the equal-size control.
- More experimental results are needed? Yes (complete equal-size control, plate-grouped split, stronger cascade variants, per-species precision, tuned or validated tiny-object variants).
- A comparative evaluation is needed? Partial. Same-campaign controls exist, but there is no comparison with other species-level insect detectors, no tile-trained SAHI, and no alternative single-stage detector family beyond a single-seed YOLO11m/RT-DETR.
- The paper needs a more critical discussion? Partial. Limitations are enumerated, but the label-origin threat and the trivially expected localized gains are not discussed critically.
- The figures are adequate? Partial. Only one figure (confusion matrices); no image samples, size distributions, PR curves or qualitative errors, which would help a reader (not fully verifiable visually).
- The conclusions and future work are convincing? Partial/No. Conclusions are hedged but the practitioner recommendation and "limited information" reading outrun the evidence.
- The references are up-to-date and appropriate? Partial. Mostly appropriate and recent; some key claims rest on an unreviewed arXiv preprint; missing literature on two-stage small-object/fine-grained recognition.
- The formatting needs adjustment? Partial. Dense tables and abstract; page limit respected (12 pp).
- The English needs improvement? Partial. Understandable, but long, heavily hedged sentences; could be tightened.

## 9. Comments to the authors
Your candor is the best asset of this paper; keep it. To make it publishable: (1) fix the contradictory statements (equal-size control, number of backbones/classifiers, which classifier is primary); (2) establish label provenance and audit the confident errors with an expert before attributing the ceiling to image information; (3) give the equal-size control enough power and run it on the localized metrics; (4) strengthen the cascade with at least one sensible variant (proposal-trained classifier, species-only relabelling, margin ablation, true-score fusion) or restrict the claim to "this recipe" in the title and RQ2; (5) validate your explanation for RQ3 with one experiment where the aerial methods should help; (6) report per-species precision and recall, and drop or replace the word "accuracy"; (7) report the omitted heads; (8) trim the repeated hedging and reallocate space to figures (sample images, size distribution, qualitative error crops); (9) release predictions and splits so that results can be checked without the images.

## 10. Confidential comments to the Program Chair
No quality issues of ethics or plagiarism are visible. The paper builds closely on a prior paper cited anonymously (same dataset lineage, same problem, headline numbers reused as motivation, and a claim of "irreducible" confusion that is tested here); the chair may want to verify that the overlap with that publication (which appears to be accepted/published) is within INSTICC self-plagiarism rules. Anonymity is otherwise maintained, but the prior paper's characterization makes the authorship guessable. There is no AI-use statement in the acknowledgements; if AI assistance was used for text, a declaration is required. My recommendation is Reject (2+); it could become Borderline with a strong rebuttal on label provenance and plate-level leakage.
